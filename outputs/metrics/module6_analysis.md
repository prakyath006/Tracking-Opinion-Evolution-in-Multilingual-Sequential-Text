### What the numbers say

- **attention_only** (attention without Bi-LSTM (attention over raw embeddings)): sentiment F1 delta +0.0614, trajectory F1 delta +0.2165.
- **lstm_only** (Bi-LSTM without attention (final hidden state only)): sentiment F1 delta +0.0952, trajectory F1 delta +0.1222.
- **mbert_sentence** (no sequential modeling (independent per-review mBERT classification)): sentiment F1 delta +0.0492, trajectory F1 delta N/A.
- **textcnn** (no transformer, no sequential modeling (word embeddings + CNN)): sentiment F1 delta +0.1058, trajectory F1 delta N/A.
- **xlmr_sentence** (no sequential modeling (independent per-review XLM-R classification)): sentiment F1 delta -0.0859, trajectory F1 delta N/A.

On Amazon, all four Group A/B architectural baselines lag the full model on
sentiment F1, by margins ranging from +0.0492 (mbert_sentence) to +0.1058
(textcnn) — textcnn, the only non-transformer, non-sequential baseline, lags
the most, which is the expected ordering. `attention_only` beats `lstm_only`
on trajectory F1 delta (+0.2165 vs +0.1222 gap to the full model, i.e.
`lstm_only` is closer to the full model — lstm_only actually scores a higher
raw trajectory F1 than attention_only, 0.4250 vs 0.3307, see
`outputs/metrics/results_table.md`), suggesting that for Amazon's opinion
trajectories, chronological recurrence (Bi-LSTM) captures more signal than
unordered self-attention alone. SCS does separate the full model from the
ablations, but not always in the full model's favor: `lstm_only`'s SCS
(0.8925) is far *higher* than the full model's (0.5636), which is a case of
near-collapse to one dominant predicted label rather than genuinely more
reliable sequential reasoning — `lstm_only`'s much lower sentiment (0.4377)
and trajectory (0.4250 vs full model's 0.5471) F1 alongside a near-1.0-range
SCS confirms this is a trivial-consistency artifact of the metric, not a
real strength (see the SCS caveat in
`outputs/metrics/final_baseline_vs_proposed_comparison.md`). Worth stating plainly rather than letting the negative sign speak for itself:
`xlmr_sentence`'s delta above (-0.0859 F1) means it *beats* the full model on
Amazon sentiment, on both accuracy (0.7800 vs 0.6700) and F1 (0.6188 vs
0.5329), even at matched (0 trainable layers) encoder capacity — the full
model's advantage on Amazon is not "wins every comparison," it is winning
against the ablations and losing to the stronger sentence-level transformer.

### What the numbers say

- **attention_only** (attention without Bi-LSTM (attention over raw embeddings)): sentiment F1 delta +0.0009, trajectory F1 delta +0.0331.
- **lstm_only** (Bi-LSTM without attention (final hidden state only)): sentiment F1 delta +0.0996, trajectory F1 delta +0.0055.
- **mbert_sentence** (no sequential modeling (independent per-review mBERT classification)): sentiment F1 delta -0.0268, trajectory F1 delta N/A.
- **textcnn** (no transformer, no sequential modeling (word embeddings + CNN)): sentiment F1 delta +0.0416, trajectory F1 delta N/A.
- **xlmr_sentence** (no sequential modeling (independent per-review XLM-R classification)): sentiment F1 delta +0.0261, trajectory F1 delta N/A.

On Tamil, `attention_only` is nearly indistinguishable from the full model on
sentiment F1 (delta +0.0009 — a 0.0009 gap, effectively a tie), which did not
hold on Amazon (+0.0614 there); code-mixed Dravidian YouTube comments appear
to compress the gap between the full architecture and a simpler
attention-only baseline more than English product reviews do. `mbert_sentence`
is again the standout: its delta is *negative* (-0.0268), meaning it beats
the full model on Tamil sentiment F1 (0.4198 vs 0.3930) as well as accuracy
(0.5219 vs 0.5134) — the same pattern as `xlmr_sentence` on Amazon, a
sentence-level transformer with no sequential modeling out-performing the
full model on raw per-review sentiment in both domains tested, just with a
different winning encoder per domain. `lstm_only` again shows the SCS
trivial-consistency pattern seen on Amazon: its Tamil SCS (0.6585) is more
than double the full model's (0.3062), while its sentiment F1 (0.2933) is the
worst of any model in this table and its trajectory F1 (0.2624) trails the
full model's (0.2678) — high consistency, low correctness. Trajectory F1 is
the one metric where the full model wins outright over both sequence
ablations in both domains (Amazon 0.5471 vs 0.4250/0.3307; Tamil 0.2678 vs
0.2624/0.2347), which is consistent with the full model's Bi-LSTM+attention
combination being better tuned for classifying an entire opinion trajectory
than either component alone, even where it loses on single-review sentiment
classification.
